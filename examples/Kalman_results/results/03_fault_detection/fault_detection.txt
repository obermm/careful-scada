MAIN-BEARING FAULT DETECTION - Kalman thermal networks, CARE Wind Farm B
=========================================================================
Code   : experiments/fault_detection.py (indicators, SLURM: runners/fd_job.sh), experiments/fault_eval.py (alarm routines, tables)
Data   : full series (train+test) of healthy 2, 21, 52, 74, 82, 83 and fault datasets 77, 53 (Rotor Bearing 2
         damage), 27 (main bearing, side unknown). Events: 77 2023-06-01, 53 2023-12-26, 27 2023-09-01.
Models : demo   = mb2-out mb2-gb1 mb1-mb2 mb1-out mb1-gb1 | rot P | c g
         simple = mb1-gb1 mb1-out mb2-gb1 mb2-out | -ws rot2 |          (8 par)
         medium = simple + P                                             (10 par)
Fit    : shared physics on the pooled healthy turbines, leave-one-turbine-out (a healthy turbine is never in its
         own fit; faults use all 6), free-run MSE, noise q/r by Kalman NLL; then per turbine refit on its first
         90 days (L2 prior 1, as KalmanHeatDetector). No alarms in the first 90 days.
         ("kalman" = all-NLL fit was also run: same picture, see ~/.cache/care_faultdet/r2/eval.csv)
Indicators (daily means, per bearing):
  heat  Kalman-tracked steady-state excess heat (library track_heat, both bearings assimilated)
  res   free-run residual (measured - simulated, no bearing assimilated)
  fric  NEW: friction-gain tracker - joint Kalman tracking of a constant heat c and an extra heat g*rot^2 per
        bearing (random walks); fric = steady excess of g at full load (load-dependent fault heat only)
Signal sets: per bearing | mb2-mb1 (both signs) | xreg = bearing - alpha*other (alpha by regression on days 30-90)
Thresholds: maximum of the statistic over the healthy turbines (leave-one-turbine-out for healthy ones);
         margin 1.15 = 15 % above that.
Metrics: FA = healthy turbines (of 6) with any alarm; wrong = fault datasets alarmed on the healthy bearing first;
         lead = event start - first alarm on the damaged bearing (days, >0 = early; - = no alarm);
         ramp = share of synthetic ramps detected within 90 d (6 turbines x 2 bearings x 3 starts = 36 each),
         0 -> A K over 30 d then held, added to ONE bearing; offset = constant, load = proportional to rot^2
         (low-passed 3 h, same mean); delay3K = median days to alarm for 3 K offset ramps.
         Noise: ramp rates +/-0.08 (binomial SE), FA from only 6 turbines -> differences < ~0.15 not significant.

## Failure analysis of the existing detectors (D1/D2: rise + level CUSUM, lagged rolling-median baseline)
1. Slow faults are absorbed: the level baseline (median of days t-90..t-30) follows the months-long rise of 77
   (heat mb2 +5 K from 2022-11 to 2023-06); the rise CUSUM needs ~5 K of net increments (threshold).
2. Thresholds are set by healthy seasonal misfit integrated by the CUSUM (level thresholds 100-200 K*day); one
   healthy turbine (ds 2, both bearings +3-4 K in winter/spring 2023) dominates; it then alarms itself (LOO).
3. Alarms right after the burn-in (tracker transient, baseline still contains the start-up).
4. Per-bearing signals carry drifts common to both bearings (82, 83, 74); asymmetry cancels them, but a plain
   mb2-mb1 inherits the full noise of the other bearing -> xreg (alpha < 1) is better.
5. The fleet does not help: healthy indicators of different turbines are almost uncorrelated (r = 0.1-0.4), so
   subtracting the fleet median does not reduce the noise (tested).
New routines: fixed per-turbine baseline (median of days 30-90), 14-day trailing mean, 7 consecutive days above
threshold (D3, D4); or 14-day mean minus the median of the 90 days ending 60 days ago (D5). Plus friction tracker (D6).

## Conclusions
* New fixed-baseline xreg detectors (D3-D5) beat the existing CUSUMs on every network: 77 detected 55-118 d before
  the event (existing: -33..+10 d), 53 at the event (0-5 d; its rise only starts ~2 weeks before), 27 ~3 months
  before (ambiguous: 27's bearing-1 indicator rises from 2023-06; not labelled), 1 FA turbine at margin 1.0,
  0 at margin 1.15 for the best combinations (D3 demo/medium, D4 simple). No wrong-bearing alarms.
* Ramp sensitivity is limited by the healthy slow drift of the indicators (~1 K std of 14-day means, single
  turbines up to 2-4 K): 1 K ramps are not detectable (<= 22 %), 2 K ~ 40-60 %, 3 K ~ 70-90 % within 90 days
  (median delay ~ 35 d, i.e. once the ramp is complete).
* Network: no consistent ranking - demo and simple are on par, medium is slightly worse; better prediction RMSE
  (medium) does not give better detection, the indicator's slow drift (not its RMSE) decides.
* Free-run residual (D4/D5) >= Kalman heat (D3); the Kalman heat tracker mainly adds smoothing.
* Friction tracker (D6): excellent for load-proportional ramps (simple: 72 % at 2 K, 97 % at 3 K) but blind to
  offsets and misses 53 and 27; healthy turbines show large load-dependent drifts (ds 2, 21) -> not recommended
  alone; real faults are a mix of load-dependent (77, 53: high-load gap grows ~3x faster) and offset heating.
* Recommended: simple network + D4 (free-run residual, xreg, fixed baseline, 14-d mean, 7-d persistence), or
  D3 with the Kalman heat tracker; threshold margin ~1.15 for zero false alarms on the 6 healthy turbines.
